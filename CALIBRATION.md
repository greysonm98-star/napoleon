# Calibration check: d'Erlon's attack, Waterloo, 18 June 1815

One historical action run through the full model, to see where the simulation agrees
with the event and where it does not. Nothing in the data files was changed to make
it fit.

Scenario: `scenarios/derlon.json` (generated by `tools/gen-derlon.mjs`).
Reproduce: `npm run napsim -- batch scenarios/derlon.json --runs 100 --out out/derlon`
then `node tools/derlon-report.mjs out/derlon`.

## The verdict in brief

- **The outcome is right.** The attack is repulsed in 94% of runs; it was repulsed.
- **The mechanism is wrong.** Historically the columns reached the crest, pushed
  back Bijlandt's brigade, were checked by Picton's infantry, and were then broken by
  the Union Brigade's charge. In the simulation two Allied batteries firing canister
  break most of the French battalions on the slope before they arrive. Picton's two
  British brigades hardly fire a shot.
- **The massed column matters, as the histories say.** Giving the French the
  formation they actually used, which could not form square, doubles their prisoners
  and raises their loss from 31% to 42%.
- **The cavalry's fate is roughly right.** The Union Brigade charges, goes on too
  far and is wrecked, as it was. In the simulation the grand battery's canister does
  the wrecking; historically it was lancers and cuirassiers.

## What is modelled

The centre of the attack, at full battalion scale. The clock starts at 13:00.

| | Force | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| French infantry | Donzelot's division, 9 battalions, 5,130; Marcognet's, 8 battalions, 4,080 | In massed divisional columns: the `french_massed` doctrine, which takes 600 s to form square or deploy |
| French guns | Five batteries, 30 guns (two of 12-pdrs) | The share of the grand battery opposite these two divisions |
| French cavalry | 4 squadrons of cuirassiers (Travers), 5 of lancers (Jacquinot) | In reserve on either flank |
| Allied infantry on the crest | Bijlandt's brigade, 5 battalions, 2,400; conscript and militia quality | |
| Allied infantry behind it | Kempt's brigade, 4 battalions, 1,923; Pack's, 4 battalions, 1,707; veteran | 130 m behind the crest, out of sight of the French guns |
| Allied guns | Rogers's and Bijleveld's batteries, 12 guns | On the crest |
| Allied cavalry | Union Brigade, 9 squadrons, 1,260 | In reserve 350 m behind the crest |

Left out: Quiot's and Durutte's divisions, La Haye Sainte and its garrison,
Papelotte, the Household Brigade and the cuirassiers it met, Best's and Vincke's
brigades, and Picton's own fall.

All eight deeper-model features are on. Ground is mud; sky clear; light wind.

**Written orders.** French: the guns fire from 13:00; both divisions hold; at 13:25
the corps commander orders both to attack in column on the crest; the cavalry is in
reserve. Allied: defend; the Union Brigade is in reserve, with a standing order to
charge when the enemy comes within 120 m of Bijlandt's brigade.

**How sure the inputs are.** Strengths, positions and timings are from memory of
Adkin and Barbero, partly confirmed by web-search summaries (Donzelot about 5,100 to
5,300 in nine battalions, Marcognet about 4,000 to 4,200 in eight, a grand battery of
80 to 84 guns in all). The British battalion strengths, Bijlandt's 2,400 and the
Union Brigade's 1,260 were not confirmed. Treat every strength as within 10 to 15%.

## What happened, as far as I can establish

| | Historical | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Result | Attack repulsed; the French fell back to their start line | Certain |
| Timing | Bombardment from about 13:00; infantry stepped off between 13:20 and 14:00 depending on the account; cavalry charge about 14:15 to 14:30; over by about 15:00 | Sources conflict by half an hour |
| Bijlandt's brigade | Gave way as the columns reached the crest | Well attested; how completely is disputed |
| Picton's brigades | Fired and counter-charged at the crest; engaged, moderate loss | Well attested |
| French loss | 2,000 to 3,000 prisoners and two eagles from the corps of about 17,000 to 19,000; killed and wounded not found | Prisoner counts conflict; no total found |
| Union Brigade | Rode on to the grand battery, was counter-charged by lancers and cuirassiers, and lost somewhere around 40 to 50% | From memory; not confirmed |

Scaled to the two divisions modelled (about half the corps's infantry), the
prisoners would be roughly 1,000 to 1,600.

## What the simulation does

100 runs, seeds 1 to 100. Losses are medians.

| | Simulation | Historical | Agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ridge held by the Allies at the end | 94% of runs | Yes | Good |
| Attack order to first French rout | Ordered 13:25, in effect at the brigades about 13:34, first battalion breaks 13:49, last 14:02 | Stepped off 13:20 to 14:00, broken by about 14:30 | Fair; the simulated repulse is quicker |
| French battalions that broke | 16 of 17 | Most of both divisions | Good |
| French infantry loss | 42% (10th to 90th percentile 31 to 50%): 24% killed and wounded, 14% prisoners, 3% dispersed | Not established; prisoners alone about 11 to 17% | Prisoners in range |
| French prisoners | About 1,300 | About 1,000 to 1,600 for these divisions | Good |
| Bijlandt's brigade | 2 of 5 battalions break; 50% loss, mostly dispersed | Gave way | Fair |
| Kempt and Pack | No loss in the median run; 5% at the 90th percentile | Engaged at the crest | **Poor** |
| Union Brigade | All 9 squadrons break; 23% killed and wounded; the rest leave the field | About 40 to 50% loss; spent as a force | Fair |
| French cavalry | 2% loss | Engaged against the British cavalry | **Poor** |
| What broke the French battalions | Canister 11.4 per run, melee 3.0, charged while forming 0.9 | Musketry at the crest and the cavalry charge | **Poor** |

"Dispersed" means the unit left the field or fell below a tenth of its strength. It
is not a casualty figure. The Union Brigade's squadrons mostly rout northward off a
map that ends 250 m behind where they started.

## Where it diverges, and what that says about the model

**1. Canister decides the attack on the slope.** Two batteries break eleven
battalions a run. Each battery fires at one battalion until it routs, a few salvos,
then turns to the next. A battalion column breaks at about 16% loss to canister, and
the climb through the last 350 m takes about ten minutes in mud, long enough for the
batteries to fire every canister round they carry. In the three runs I traced,
Allied canister hit 1,000 to 1,150 men (about 4.5 a round) and Allied roundshot 130
to 170, of 9,200. That is probably too many, and it is also concentrated on one
battalion at a time, with each battalion breaking on its own. Real batteries fired
into the mass in front of them, and a battalion inside a divisional column could
not easily leave it.
This is the same sensitivity the Phase 2 sweeps found, here with a historical
case against it.

Halving and quartering the canister factor (ordinary columns, 60 runs each):

| Canister factor | Ridge held | Kempt and Pack loss | French routs by canister, by melee |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 (as shipped) | 98% | 0% | 11.6, 2.4 per run |
| 0.05 | 73% | 17% | 7.0, 6.7 |
| 0.025 | 58% | 31% | 4.1, 9.0 |

At half the shipped value the action looks more like the record: the columns reach
the crest, Picton's brigades fight, and the result is in doubt. That is evidence the
shipped factor (0.1, chosen to match the prototype) is too high by something like a
factor of two. I have not changed it: one action is thin grounds, and the fault may
equally lie in targeting or in how battalions in a mass share loss.

**2. Picton's infantry is a spectator.** A consequence of the first point, and of
the infantry standing on the reverse slope with nothing to make it come forward
until a brigadier's initiative fires.

**3. The cavalry charge is not the decisive blow.** The Union Brigade does charge
(on Ponsonby's initiative or on the standing order; in the run I traced the two came
within seconds of each other) and does ride down battalions caught unable to form square: about one a run,
plus the prisoners. But most battalions have already been broken by the guns.

**4. The French cavalry does little.** The British squadrons are broken by the grand
battery's canister before the lancers reach them. In the model cavalry prefers guns
as a target, charges them frontally, and blown cavalry sits in front of the battery.
The overreach is reproduced; the counter-charge is not.

**5. No shot passes through one unit into the next.** A deep mass is no worse a
target for roundshot than a single battalion column. The bombardment therefore does
little (Bijlandt's brigade is hurt mainly in the fight at the crest).

## The massed column, tested

The histories blame the formation: battalions deployed in line and stacked a few
paces apart, unable to form square. The same scenario with ordinary battalion columns
(`scenarios/derlon-ordinary-columns.json`, 100 runs, same seeds):

| | Massed columns (historical) | Ordinary columns |
|---|---|---|
| Ridge held | 94% | 98% |
| French infantry loss | 42% | 31% |
| of which prisoners | 14% | 6% |
| French battalions broken while forming, per run | 0.9 | 0.3 |
| Union Brigade squadrons broken | 9 of 9 | 7 of 9 |

In ordinary columns the battalions form square in 45 seconds and the cavalry
recoils from them. In the massed formation they are caught, and prisoners more than
double. The direction and rough size agree with the standard account. (The Allies
hold the ridge slightly less often against the massed columns, because the British
cavalry spends itself more completely.)

## Which features matter here

Each feature switched off in turn (ordinary columns, 60 runs each; all on gives
ridge held 98%, French infantry loss 31%, Bijlandt 66%):

| Switched off | Ridge held | Most visible change |
|---|---|---|
| Command and control | 13% | With no orders to wait for, everything attacks at once from the first minute, cavalry included, and the Allies are swamped in half an hour |
| Weather and ground (firm, not mud) | 93% | French loss rises to 43% with 15% prisoners; the action is shorter |
| Fatigue | 92% | French loss 35% |
| Skirmishers | 95% | Kempt and Pack take 6% |
| Quality | 100% | Bijlandt's militia stands like line troops: loss falls from 66% to 35% |
| Smoke | 100% | French loss 28% |
| Ammunition | 98% | Bijlandt's loss rises to 79% |
| Line of sight | 98% | Little: the British brigades are not being shot at either way |

Command and control changes the result more than everything else together, because
it is what holds the attack back for the bombardment and keeps the reserves in
reserve. Quality is what makes Bijlandt's brigade give way.

## What I would look at next

In order of how much the evidence above points at them:

1. How artillery chooses and holds targets, and how a battalion inside a larger mass
   shares loss and morale with its neighbours.
2. The canister factor, tested against a second historical action before it is changed.
3. Roundshot passing through to units behind.
4. Cavalry's preference for charging guns frontally, and what blown cavalry does next.
5. A rule that brings reverse-slope infantry forward to the crest when the enemy nears it.
